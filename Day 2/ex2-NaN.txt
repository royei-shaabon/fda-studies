There are a few real strategies for handling this, and picking the right one matters:

Skip/filter the bad record — if one product is malformed, drop it from the array rather than poisoning your dataset. Good when a few bad records are acceptable to lose.
Fall back to a default value — e.g. treat malformed price as 0 or null. Risky if that default gets silently treated as real data (a "$0 product" might look like a legitimate free item).
Throw/reject the record explicitly — force the caller to know this record failed, rather than quietly returning a broken object.
Return null/undefined for that specific field, and make your Product type honestly reflect that the field might be missing — pushing the "what do I do about it" decision to wherever the data is actually used.

There's no single universally "correct" choice — it depends on what you're building. For something like an investment-ranking agent (hint, hint), silently defaulting a broken number to 0 could badly skew a ranking. Being explicit about failure is usually safer than guessing.
